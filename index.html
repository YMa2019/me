A New Framework for Bayesian Function Registration
Yijia Ma, Wei Wu
Electronic Journal of Statistics, 2025

This project introduces a Bayesian function registration framework that places stochastic-process priors directly on time-warping functions, improving alignment accuracy, posterior inference, and uncertainty quantification for temporally misaligned functional data.

Bayesian modeling, MCMC, stochastic processes, functional data analysis, prior constraints

webpage | abstract | paper | code

Abstract. Function registration, also known as alignment, is a fundamental problem in functional data analysis. Classical registration methods, such as Fisher-Rao alignment, focus on estimating optimal time-warping functions between curves. Recent Bayesian approaches model time warping directly by combining a prior on warping functions with a classical registration criterion as the likelihood term. These Bayesian methods have shown improvements over classical approaches. However, existing prior models often rely on nonlinear approximations, which may introduce inaccuracy and computational inefficiency. To address these limitations, we propose a new Bayesian framework that uses a linear representation of time-warping functions, allowing flexible Gaussian or non-Gaussian stochastic-process priors. This approach avoids linearization approximation in time-warping computation and obtains posterior inference through conventional Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods. Through multiple simulation studies, we investigate how the choice of prior affects registration performance and demonstrate that the proposed framework outperforms previous methods. We also apply the method to a real dataset and obtain desirable alignment results.
A Stochastic Process Representation for Time Warping Functions
Yijia Ma, Xinyu Zhou, Wei Wu
Computational Statistics & Data Analysis, 2024

This project develops a stochastic-process framework for modeling nonlinear time-warping functions, enabling flexible simulation, penalized functional alignment, uncertainty-aware phase-variation analysis, and outlier detection in complex temporal data.

Hilbert space, stochastic processes, functional data analysis, penalized function registration, functional boxplots

webpage | abstract | paper | code

Abstract. Time-warping functions provide a mathematical representation for measuring phase variability in functional data. Although recent studies have developed various approaches to estimate optimal warpings between functions, a principled, linear, and generative representation of time-warping functions remains under-explored because warping functions are nonlinear in the conventional L2 space. To address this problem, we define a new linear warping space and propose a stochastic-process representation to characterize time-warping functions. The key idea is to define an inner-product structure on the warping space, followed by a transformation that maps warping functions into a subspace of L2. Under certain constraints, this transformation is an isometric isomorphism. In the transformed space, L2 basis functions in a Hilbert space can be used to generate time-warping functions through different types of stochastic processes. The effectiveness of this representation is demonstrated by using it as a new penalty in penalized function registration, together with an efficient gradient method for minimizing the cost function. The proposed penalized method is illustrated through simulations that characterize nonuniform and correlated constraints in the time domain. This representation is also used to develop boxplots for warping functions, estimate templates, and identify warping outliers. Finally, the method is applied to a COVID-19 dataset to construct boxplots and identify states with outlying growth patterns.

Let EEG Models Learn EEG
Yifan Wang, Yijia Ma, Wen Li, Chenyu You
ICML, 2026

This project develops principled constraints for EEG representation learning, helping EEG models better capture signal structure and improve model generalization for neural time-series analysis.

EEG, representation learning, neural time series, machine learning, model constraints

webpage | abstract | paper | code

Abstract. High-fidelity EEG generation is critical for alleviating data scarcity and addressing privacy constraints in large-scale neural modeling. Despite recent progress, most existing approaches formulate EEG generation via discrete denoising objectives, which inadequately reflect the inherently continuous temporal dynamics and spectral structure of neural activity. As a result, these methods often struggle to preserve long-range temporal dependencies and exhibit mismatches in the spectral and temporal structure of the generated signals. In this work, we argue that effective EEG generation requires models that operate directly on the continuous evolution of neural signals. We introduce Just EEG Transformer (JET), a generative framework based on conditional flow matching that models EEG as raw sequences evolving along continuous trajectories. By learning a smooth vector field that transports noise to the EEG data distribution, JET captures temporal continuity and transient dynamics without relying on discretized denoising schemes or domain-specific representations. To ensure that the learned dynamics remain consistent with key properties of EEG signals, we introduce principled constraints that preserve spectral structure, temporal stationarity, and signal-level statistics. Across three large-scale benchmarks, JET consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance, reducing TS-FID by over 40% compared to strong baselines. Extensive analyses show that JET captures key structural properties of neural dynamics, providing a scalable and principled approach to EEG generation.
